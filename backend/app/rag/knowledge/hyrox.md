## Hyrox Training Methodology

Hyrox rewards the ability to run well *between* strength-endurance stations, not just
strength or running in isolation. Training should alternate compromised running (running
immediately after a fatiguing station, e.g. 1km run straight after sled pushes) with
station-specific strength endurance, so the body adapts to producing running economy
under residual fatigue - this is the single biggest gap in athletes who train running
and strength separately but never combined.

## Sled Push and Pull Programming

Sled push performance is driven by leg drive and the ability to maintain a low, consistent
push angle over the full distance rather than short bursts of maximal force.
Programming heavy sled pushes (moderate distance, near-maximal load, 4-6 sets) once
per week alongside lighter, faster technique-focused pushes on a second day builds
both the strength and the pacing needed for race-day loads. Sled pulls benefit similarly
from a mix of heavy short pulls and lighter continuous pulls for grip and back endurance.

## Endurance Programming for Hyrox

A Hyrox-specific base should include both continuous zone 2 running (building the
aerobic engine that recovers heart rate between stations) and interval work matched to
race demands - e.g. 8km compromised runs broken into 1km blocks with a strength-endurance
exercise between each, mimicking race structure directly rather than training running and
strength as separate blocks.

## Strength Endurance Principles

Unlike bodybuilding-style hypertrophy training, Hyrox strength endurance work favors
higher rep ranges (12-20+) at moderate loads with limited rest, aiming to build local
muscular endurance in the exact movement patterns used in competition (wall balls,
lunges, farmers carries) rather than pure maximal strength, which has diminishing
returns once a reasonable strength base is established.

## Wall Balls and Burpee Broad Jumps

Both wall balls and burpee broad jumps are pacing challenges as much as strength
challenges - athletes who go out too fast see rep quality (and no-rep risk) collapse in
the final third. Training should include sets performed at planned race pace (not
maximal effort) for the full rep count, so pacing becomes automatic rather than
something worked out mid-race.
