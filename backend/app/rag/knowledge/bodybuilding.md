## Hypertrophy Principles

Muscle growth responds primarily to total weekly volume (sets taken close to failure)
per muscle group, progressive overload over time, and adequate protein intake -
roughly 10-20 hard sets per muscle group per week is a reasonable range for most
trained lifters, with beginners needing less and advanced lifters often needing more
to keep progressing. Training to within 1-3 reps of failure is sufficient; grinding
every set to complete failure adds fatigue without proportional extra growth.

## Exercise Selection for Hypertrophy

Compound lifts (squats, presses, rows, deadlift variations) are efficient for building
overall size and should form the base of a program, but isolation exercises
(leg extensions, lateral raises, curls) are valuable for targeting muscles that compound
lifts under-stimulate, and for pushing volume on a specific muscle without the systemic
fatigue cost of adding more compound sets. A mix of both, not one exclusively, produces
the best results for most people.

## Training Split Considerations

Splitting training so each muscle group is trained roughly twice per week generally
outperforms training it once per week at the same total volume, because it spreads the
stimulus and recovery demand more evenly - this matters more as weekly volume per
muscle group increases; at low volumes, split frequency matters less.

## Nutrition Fundamentals for Muscle Gain

A modest calorie surplus (roughly 200-300 kcal above maintenance) supports muscle gain
while limiting excess fat gain; larger surpluses speed weight gain but mostly add fat,
not muscle. Protein intake of about 1.6-2.2g per kg of bodyweight per day is sufficient
for maximizing muscle protein synthesis for most people - intakes well above this range
provide little additional benefit for hypertrophy specifically.

## Combining Hypertrophy Training with a Field Sport

When training for size alongside a sport like football, prioritize lower-body hypertrophy
work for muscles that also support sprinting and change of direction (posterior chain,
quads) and schedule the heaviest resistance sessions away from high-intensity match days
so residual soreness doesn't compromise sport performance. Total training volume should
flex down during heavy match periods rather than staying fixed.
